<objective>
Harden the two shutdown primitives plan 04 composes so that reusing them actually establishes KILL-01 and KILL-02. Today `Pty::kill_and_wait` returns `Ok(())` as soon as the direct child has exited or its identity no longer matches, without signalling or waiting on the recorded process group, so a descendant that outlived its shell leader survives removal. The reader thread is spawned detached and nothing joins it, so a successful kill leaves a thread and open terminal handles whose closure nobody owns. This plan fixes both inside the primitives, where every caller benefits, and pins the pid-reuse rule as a pure, table-tested decision.

Purpose: cross-AI review HIGH findings 6 and 7 (REVIEWS.md L125-L126@0070c74). KILL-01 says the whole session process group is gone, KILL-02 says it is reaped; neither can be proven by the orchestration layer if the primitive stops early. Business logic with crisp I/O (process state, errno, thread completion): TDD.
Output: `GroupDisposition` and `group_disposition`, `Pty::reader_finished`, the retained reader `JoinHandle`, the extinction path for an exited leader, a `Session::kill_and_wait` contract doc comment, and four tests.
</objective>
